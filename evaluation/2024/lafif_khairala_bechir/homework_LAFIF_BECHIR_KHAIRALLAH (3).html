Question 2: describe the dataset. Intuitively, which variables would you associate with a higher disutility of unemployment?

This dataset comprises survey responses covering various aspects of respondents’ lives, including their subjective health, satisfaction with life, opinions on work, and socio-demographic details such as gender, age, marital status, education level, employment status, and household income.

  • Satisfaction with your life: Individuals who are more satisfied with their lives are likely to experience a higher disutility from being unemployed, as unemployment can significantly impact their overall well-being and life satisfaction.
  • Humiliating to receive money without having to work for it: This variable directly captures the psychological disutility that individuals may experience from receiving money without having to work for it, which is often associated with unemployment.
  • Work is a duty towards society: Individuals who strongly believe that work is a duty towards society are likely to experience a higher disutility from being unemployed, as they may feel that they are not fulfilling their societal obligations.

Why relative_income is Better than Raw Income: Normalization: Normalization of the income data is carried out in the different countries; it makes sure there is an adjustment for the economic difference. For example, the income in one country can be high and in another, average to low because of the different living costs or conditions. Comparative analysis: It allows the researcher to make relatively more accurate comparative estimates of differences in financial well-being between individual respondents from different countries represented in the data set. Economic context: It shows the relative economic status of the persons within their own national context and is more relevant to most kinds of social and economic analysis than absolute income numbers.
